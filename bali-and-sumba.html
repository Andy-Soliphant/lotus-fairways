There are not many places left where a serious hotel sits on a beach with almost nothing else around it. Sumba is one. It is an hour’s flight east of Bali, larger than Bali, with a fraction of its visitors, and a culture of thatched clan villages and megalithic tombs that is still lived in rather than performed.

This journey uses Bali as the bookends: four nights in the Ubud hills to begin, two on the clifftops before the flight home. Sumba is the middle, and the reason for the trip. There are two very different places to stay there, and we will tell you which suits you.

The Journey

1Ubud

The Valley — Four Nights

Ninety minutes from the airport to Amandari, laid out like a village above the Ayung gorge. Tirta Empul early, the rice terraces of Jatiluwih, a cooking morning in a family compound, and time to do nothing at all before the island.

2Sumba

The Island — Four Nights

An hour’s flight to Tambolaka. Choose the house that fits the trip. Nihi Sumba sits on its own beach on the south-west coast, full board, famous for its surf and its horses, and about as remote as luxury gets in Asia. Cap Karoso is on the west coast near the Weekuri lagoon, younger, lighter on the pocket and closer to the old villages. From either: a day among the high-roofed clan houses and stone tombs of the west coast villages, such as Ratenggaro; a ride along the sand at low tide; and a day on the water.

The Clifftops — Two Nights

Back to Bali and up onto the Bukit, to Alila Villas Uluwatu, its villas set along the clifftop above the Indian Ocean. Uluwatu temple and the Kecak dance at sunset, dinner on the sand at Jimbaran, and a short drive to the airport.

Nihi or Cap Karoso?

The honest answer is that they are different trips. Nihi is the one people cross the world for: full board, its own beach, and a price to match. Cap Karoso is newer, more design-led, more relaxed about it, and costs a good deal less. The route above is priced with Nihi; the Cap Karoso version starts from £3,800 per person.
