Most people who come home disappointed by Bali saw the version between the airport and the beach clubs: traffic, crowds and a resort that could be anywhere. The island they were promised is still there. It is just further in.

This journey starts in the hills above the Ayung river, at the house that set the standard for luxury in Bali, moves east to a valley most visitors never reach, and finishes with four nights by the sea. Three moves in ten nights, none of them more than two hours, and every day out timed to miss the crowds rather than join them.

The Journey

1Ubud

The Valley — Four Nights

An hour and a half from the airport to Amandari, Aman’s first house in Bali, its suites laid out like a village above the Ayung gorge. A morning at Tirta Empul, where the Balinese come to bathe in the spring water, before the coaches arrive. A day west to the rice terraces at Jatiluwih, still farmed under the old subak water system and listed by UNESCO for it. And at least one day with nothing in it at all.

Tirta EmpulJatiluwihAmandari
2Sidemen

Beneath Agung — Two Nights

East, into the Sidemen valley: terraced rice, weaving villages and Mount Agung filling the end of the view. Samanvaya is adults only, built in the traditional style and looking straight across the terraces to the volcano. An early morning at Besakih, the mother temple, high on the mountain’s flank; an afternoon with the weavers of songket cloth in the village below.

BesakihSamanvaya
3Jimbaran

The Bay — Four Nights

South to the Bukit peninsula and Four Seasons Jimbaran Bay, its villas stepping down the hill above the bay, each with its own plunge pool. The beach is a short walk; the fish market at dawn is shorter. One evening at Uluwatu, the temple on its cliff above the Indian Ocean, with the Kecak dance as the sun goes down. The rest is the villa and the sea, twenty minutes from the airport on the way home.
