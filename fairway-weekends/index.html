Phuket has it all — championship courses, genuinely world-class hotels, the Andaman Sea, and the kind of evenings that golf groups return for. Bangkok — Asia's Finest Golf City

Bangkok is chronically underrated as a golf destination. Over 60 championship layouts sit within an hour of the city centre — courses that have hosted Asian Tour events and are maintained to a standard that would satisfy the most discerning player. Add one of the world's great cities as your backdrop and Bangkok becomes a Fairway Weekend in its own right, not just a stopover.

Bangkok's food scene is one of the world's best — from street-level Chinatown to rooftop restaurants above the Chao Phraya. The city's nightlife is legendary. Temples, markets and river bars for those who want something quieter. Bangkok does everything, and does it well.

Hua Hin — The Golfer's Choice

If the priority is the golf above everything else, Hua Hin is where you go. Black Mountain consistently ranks among the top ten courses in Asia — tour conditioning, extraordinary caddies and a layout that rewards every level of player. The royal town itself is relaxed and charming, two and a half hours south of Bangkok by road, making it a natural pairing with the capital for a four or five night break.

Vietnam

Da Nang — Vietnam's Golf Coast

The central Vietnamese coast has emerged as one of Asia's genuinely great golf destinations over the past decade. Hoiana Shores, designed by Robert Trent Jones II along the natural dunes, is a course that belongs in any serious golfer's life list. Ba Na Hills, Luke Donald's design in the cooler foothills above the city, offers a completely different experience. Stay at the Four Seasons The Nam Hai and spend your evenings in Hoi An — one of Southeast Asia's most beautiful towns, fifteen minutes away.

Our hotels

Best for

Golfers wanting culture alongside sport, couples, those who haven't played Vietnam

When to go

February to August. The central coast dry season is distinct from northern Vietnam.

After the round

Hoi An Ancient Town is fifteen minutes from most courses and hotels — lantern-lit streets, the Thu Bon River, the finest food in Vietnam, and the kind of evening atmosphere that stays with you long after the trip. Da Nang itself has a growing dining and bar scene along the Han River. The evenings here are genuinely special.

Vietnam

Ho Chi Minh City & The Bluffs Ho Tram

The Bluffs Ho Tram Strip — Greg Norman's design on Vietnam's southern coast — is ranked among the top 100 courses in the world. Playing it is not optional for any serious golfer who comes to Southeast Asia. Based in Saigon, either as a day trip for those with limited time or an overnight stay for those who want to experience the resort properly, The Bluffs earns the journey entirely on its own. Ho Chi Minh City itself adds one of Asia's most energetic urban experiences alongside it.

Vietnam

Hanoi — Golf in the Capital

Hanoi is less established as a golf destination than the central coast or the south — which means those who discover it find something still relatively uncrowded. Van Tri Golf Club is widely regarded as the finest course in northern Vietnam, and the surrounding region offers a range of layouts that make a four or five night golf break genuinely compelling. Combine with a night on Ha Long Bay and the trip becomes one of the most complete short breaks available anywhere in Southeast Asia.

Golf + UNESCO
Cambodia

Siem Reap — Golf in the Shadow of Angkor

Siem Reap offers one of the most extraordinary combinations in golf travel — Angkor Golf Resort, Nick Faldo's design and the finest course in Cambodia, paired with the temples of Angkor, one of the world's great archaeological sites. Playing a world-class round in the morning and visiting Angkor Wat at sunset in the same day is not something many destinations can offer. Siem Reap does it effortlessly, and at a price point that makes it exceptional value for the experience delivered.

Angkor Golf Resort, Phokeethra Country Club

Best for

Golfers wanting something genuinely different, culture alongside sport, those combining with Vietnam or Thailand

When to go

November to April. Dry season — cool mornings, clear skies, ideal playing conditions.

After the round

Angkor Wat at sunrise or sunset is an experience that transcends the itinerary. Beyond the main temple, the jungle-covered ruins of Ta Prohm and the Bayon's carved faces reward those who explore. Siem Reap town has a good restaurant scene and a lively bar strip — the Pub Street area is unpretentious and well-suited to golf groups winding down after a long day. The Old Market area offers better dining.

Cambodia

Phnom Penh — Cambodia's Emerging Capital

Phnom Penh has quietly become one of Southeast Asia's most interesting cities — its food scene, its riverfront setting and its complex history told with remarkable honesty. The golf is not Phokeethra's standard, but Cambodia Golf & Country Club offers a solid championship layout that more than justifies a round or two. Combine with Siem Reap for a full Cambodia golf break, or use Phnom Penh as a standalone destination for groups who want culture and city life as much as golf.

Rosewood Phnom Penh, Hyatt Regency Phnom Penh, Raffles Hotel Le Royal, The Plantation Urban Resort

Best for

Siem Reap combinations, culture-first groups, those wanting an authentic Southeast Asian capital

When to go

November to April. Phnom Penh is year-round but dry season offers the most comfortable conditions.

After the round

The Phnom Penh riverfront at night is one of the more atmospheric settings in Southeast Asia — the confluence of the Mekong and Tonle Sap rivers, lantern-lit restaurants, a bar scene that has grown considerably without losing its character. The Foreign Correspondents' Club terrace remains one of the great sunset spots in Asia. The city's food scene has developed substantially — excellent Khmer cuisine alongside strong international options.
