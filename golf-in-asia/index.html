Why Golf in Asia Changes Everything

The caddie — the real difference

A good Asian caddie is not a bagman. By the third hole they know your game. They will pass you the right club before you reach for the wrong one. They will read a putt you would have three-putted. They will find the ball you were certain was lost. After a week of playing with a skilled caddie, returning home and pushing your own trolley is a genuine psychological adjustment.

Caddie fees included in all our itineraries — no exceptions.

Conditions you can plan around

Dry season in Southeast Asia means weeks of clear skies, warm mornings and courses in peak condition. No frost holds. No waterlogged fairways. No checking the weather the night before and hoping. Tee times arranged for the best light — usually early morning, when the air is cool and the courses are quiet.

We advise on the right destination for your travel dates — the dry seasons vary by region.

Value without compromise

Championship green fees in Asia cost a fraction of equivalent courses in Europe — with a caddie included and a cart available. The courses are not lesser for being more affordable. Courses designed by the same architects who built Augusta's contemporaries, maintained to tour standard, with a level of service the best European clubs would struggle to match.

From £80 per round including caddie. Premium experiences from £150.

For Those Who Want More Than One Country

Most golfers spend their time within one country and go deep. But for those with 18 nights or more, combining two countries opens up itineraries that are genuinely extraordinary — golf and culture in combination across borders.

18–21 nights

Thailand + Vietnam

Start in Phuket or Hua Hin for the established golf infrastructure and beach setting, then fly to Da Nang for Hoiana Shores and the central coast. Hoi An in the evenings. Two completely different golf experiences in one trip.

16–18 nights

Vietnam + Cambodia

Da Nang and the central coast, then south to Ho Chi Minh City for The Bluffs Ho Tram, then across to Siem Reap for Phokeethra and Angkor. Vietnam's golf depth combined with Cambodia's extraordinary cultural backdrop.

21–28 nights

The Indochina Golf Trail

Thailand, Vietnam and Cambodia in a single journey. The full picture of Asian golf — established infrastructure, emerging coastal courses and an extraordinary final destination. For those who want the complete experience.

How We Plan a Golf Trip

We sequence courses deliberately

The best golf itineraries are not just lists of great courses — they are sequenced so that each round feels different from the last, and the trip builds rather than repeats. We know which courses to play in which order, and how to structure the days between rounds so that the non-golf time earns its place too.

We arrange tee times that work

Early morning tee times in the right weather window. Courses that suit your group's pace of play. Access to private clubs that cannot be booked independently. Thirty years of relationships with golf directors across the region means the tee times we secure are not always the ones available to individual travellers.

We match courses to players

A scratch golfer and an 18-handicapper need different courses. A group who want to score versus a group who want the experience — different priorities, different layouts. We ask the right questions before we recommend anything, and we are honest when a course does not match what a group is looking for.

We build the full trip

Hotels chosen for the morning after a long day on the course. Transfers that get you to the first tee on time. Evenings that the group will talk about as much as the rounds. Every golf trip we plan is a complete trip — not a list of tee times with hotels added as an afterthought.
