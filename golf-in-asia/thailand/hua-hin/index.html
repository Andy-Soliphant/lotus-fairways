Why Hua Hin

Hua Hin was Thailand’s first beach resort, and it still behaves like one. The royal family have summered here since the 1920s, and that single fact has shaped everything since — the town grew up quietly, on its own terms, and never turned into the sort of place you have to apologise for booking. The old railway station is still there. So is the night market. It is a working Thai town that happens to have a beach, rather than a beach that had a town built around it.

For golf, that matters more than it sounds. The courses sit inland among the hills, ten to thirty minutes from the seafront, which means you play somewhere genuinely rural and sleep somewhere with restaurants and a proper evening. You are not marooned at a resort, and you are not fighting traffic to reach a tee.

Three courses in a week is the right rhythm here. Black Mountain alone justifies the trip — and the week is routed to build to it, opening at Majestic Creek and stepping up through Pineapple Valley, with the days between the rounds the point rather than the filler. Bangkok is a straightforward drive north, and Chiang Mai is a direct flight, so Hua Hin also works as the opening or closing leg of something longer.

The Week

A suggested shape — every day flexes to your group, your handicaps and your pace. Nothing here is fixed in stone.

Day1

Arrival

Private transfer down from Bangkok, roughly three hours with a stop if you want one. Nothing is asked of you today. The night market runs the length of Dechanuchit Road and is the easiest possible introduction to the town — grilled seafood, cheap beer, and no need to have booked anything.

Day2

Majestic Creek Golf Club Round 1

The week opens easy, and that’s the strategy. Majestic Creek — newly renovated, out in the foothills — is wide, generous and forgiving of a swing still shaking off the flight. Scores tend to be good here, confidence arrives early, and the week has somewhere to build to.

Majestic Creek
Day3

At leisure

Deliberately empty, and well placed — the opening round played, the serious golf still ahead. Hua Hin rewards a slow day better than most golf destinations — the beach is long enough to walk properly, the spas are good and absurdly cheap, and the vineyards inland make a decent half-day if anyone is restless. We can arrange any of it, or none of it.

Day4

Pineapple Valley Golf Club Round 2

Pineapple Valley is the step up, and the one people are surprised by. The name is literal — laid out over old plantation land — and it rolls far more than you expect from a Thai resort course, with variety that holds your attention right through the back nine. Asia’s Best New Course of 2009, and it has aged well.

Pineapple Valley
Day5

At leisure

Another open day, and the one most groups use properly. The royal town is small enough to walk — the railway station is worth twenty minutes on its own — and the fishing pier at the north end of the beach is where to eat if you want the version of Hua Hin the guidebooks skip.

Day6

Black Mountain Country Club Round 3

The course the whole week has been building to. Black Mountain is why golfers come to Hua Hin — an Asian PGA Tour venue with tour conditioning, room off the tee and proper questions on approach. Take the early tee time for the temperature as much as the light, and let lunch at the clubhouse close the golf properly.

Black Mountain · Asian PGA Tour venue
Day7

At leisure

Yours entirely. Most groups play the morning again somewhere, or don’t, and spend the last evening on the seafront. We’ll book the table wherever you want it.

Day8

Departure

Private transfer back to either Bangkok airport, timed around your flight rather than a fixed departure. If you are continuing to Chiang Mai or the islands, we build that in instead.
