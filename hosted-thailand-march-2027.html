Most golf weeks in Thailand are sold by the coachload, with a local representative who meets you at the airport and a phone number for everything after that. This one is a single departure of twelve places, and the host travels every mile of it — the transfers, the tee times, the dinners and the rounds themselves. It is not a programme that runs every fortnight with a different group in it. It runs once.

That is the difference the price reflects. Thirty years of building golf itineraries across Asia means the courses were chosen by someone who has played them all, in the order he would play them himself, and the clubs know him by name when the group arrives. If a tee time slips, a flight is late or a round needs moving, it is sorted by the person standing beside you rather than relayed down a phone.

The week runs the right way round. Five nights in Hua Hin first, where the courses sit minutes from the beach and the pace is slow, then north to Bangkok for three nights and the two best tee times of the trip. Partners who do not play are not an afterthought: there is something worth doing on every golf day, and they pay a price that reflects the rounds they are not taking.

Departure

There is a trophy at the end of it

Every round counts. Stableford off full handicaps, points carried through all six rounds, so a poor morning on the Thursday does not end anyone's week. Nearest the pin and longest drive each day, which are the prizes the rest of us actually win. Royal Gems on the Tuesday is played as pairs better-ball alongside the individual scoring, with the pairings drawn at dinner the night before and nobody choosing their own partner — it is the day eight people stop being strangers. Partners have their own competition on the vineyard afternoon.

Scores go on a live leaderboard you can follow from the buggy, and the prizes are given out at the farewell dinner in Bangkok after Thai Country Club. The trophy is presented there and your name joins the ones already on it. You take home an engraved keepsake; the cup travels back with us and comes out again next March for whoever wants to take it off you.

Handicaps are taken from your WHS record, which keeps it honest, and we ask for it when you book.

If this would be your first time in Asia

Thailand is one of the easiest places in the world to travel, and it is still a long way from home the first time you do it. On this trip you do not arrange a transfer, work out a tip, book a tee time, find a restaurant or carry your clubs anywhere. You are met at Bangkok airport and you are not on your own again until you choose to be.

For a golfer travelling without a partner it answers the other question too. You arrive alone and you play in a three-ball every day, eat with the group when you want to and take an evening off when you don't. Eight to twelve people is enough for proper company and small enough that nobody is lost in it — and there is a single supplement rather than a stranger in the other bed.

Travelling with your host

A host who travels with you changes a golf holiday in small, practical ways. The tee sheet is arranged so the group plays together rather than being split across a morning. Someone who knows the caddie master is there when it matters. Dinner is booked by a person who has eaten there. Nobody spends an evening working out where the bus goes in the morning.

It also decides who the week suits: golf couples, pairs of friends, and single golfers who would rather join a group than organise one. Eight guests minimum and twelve maximum, so the party fits three tee times and one dinner table.

One departure, one host, and no coach.

If one of you doesn't play

Partners travel at the lower price and are part of the week, not parked for it. Some days carry an outing at no extra cost — the afternoon at the Hua Hin Hills vineyard above the town is for everyone, golfers included, and it is the rest day the whole group looks forward to. Other days there is something to choose from if you want it, arranged by your host and paid for only if you take it: a cooking class, the national park and the cave at Sam Roi Yot, the old royal palace on the shore, a day in Hoi An's equivalent on the river in Bangkok, or an afternoon doing nothing at all by the pool.

And the golf is open to you whenever you fancy it. Come out for a few holes, ride along for the afternoon, or take a tee time of your own — your host will arrange it at the course. Every dinner, every transfer and every evening is shared, because a group of ten is better company than a group of eight.

Getting there

Flights are not included, and we would rather arrange them around you than sell everyone the same seat. Some guests want the nonstop from Heathrow, some want to leave from home, some want a night in Bangkok first or a few days somewhere else afterwards. Tell us where you want to fly from and what you want from the journey, and we will come back with the options and the fares.

As a guide: Norwich connects through Amsterdam on a single ticket with your clubs checked through, Heathrow flies nonstop in around eleven and a half hours, and Manchester connects through Doha, Dubai or Amsterdam. What matters is the arrival: we meet the group at Bangkok on the morning of Wednesday 17 March, and we will tell you which arrivals work before you book anything.

If you would rather book your own, that is fine too — send us the flight numbers and we will do the rest.
