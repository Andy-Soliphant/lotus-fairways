Borobudur is little more than an hour’s flight from Bali, and most people who visit one never see the other. That is the gap this journey closes. It begins in Central Java, in front of a ninth-century temple that sat under volcanic ash and jungle for centuries, and ends on the cliffs of East Bali looking across the strait to Lombok.

In between is Ubud, but not the Ubud of the main street. The Java nights come first, while you are fresh and the early starts are easy; the last four nights are the reward.

The Journey

1Borobudur

The Temple — Three Nights

Arrive at Yogyakarta and drive into the hills to Amanjiwo, set in a crescent facing Borobudur across the rice fields, so the temple is in view from the first evening. Before dawn, up to the hill at Punthuk Setumbu to watch the sun come up behind Merapi with the stupas rising out of the mist; then onto the temple itself as it opens. Access to the upper terraces is now limited to small timed groups, and we book the places. A day in Yogyakarta for the sultan’s palace and the batik workshops, and Prambanan, the great Hindu temple of the same age, at dusk.

2Ubud

The Rainforest — Four Nights

A short flight to Bali and up into the forest at Keliki, north of Ubud, to Capella Ubud: a camp of tents designed by Bill Bensley, each with its own salt-water pool, strung along a jungle ridge. A walk through the rice fields to the village with a local guide; the water temple at Tirta Empul early, before the crowds; the terraces at Tegallalang. The camp itself is worth the time it asks for.

Tirta EmpulCapella Ubud
3Manggis

The East Coast — Four Nights

East to Amankila on the cliffs above the Lombok Strait, with its three pools stepping down towards the sea. This is older, quieter Bali: the water palace at Tirta Gangga, the walled village of Tenganan, and a morning by outrigger along the coast. Ninety minutes to the airport at the end.
