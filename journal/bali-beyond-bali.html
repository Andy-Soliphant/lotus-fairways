Bali, Beyond Bali: Java, Lombok and Sumba

Indonesia · Java · Lombok · Sumba · 7 min read

Indonesia · Beyond Bali

Almost everyone who goes to Indonesia goes to Bali, and most of them go nowhere else. That is understandable. Bali is easy to reach, it has more good hotels than anywhere else in the country, and the island at its best is still one of the great places in Asia. But Indonesia is more than seventeen thousand islands, and three of the most rewarding are each about an hour’s flight from Bali’s airport.

Java has the temples. Lombok has the beaches Bali had forty years ago. Sumba has a way of life that has barely changed in centuries, and one of the most talked-about hotels in the world. Adding one of them turns a holiday into a journey. Here is how we think about which.

The short answer

For history and architecture: Java, for Borobudur and Prambanan.

For beaches and space: Lombok, especially the south coast.

For somewhere genuinely remote: Sumba.

Three or four nights is right for any of them. Two is too few to justify the flights.

Java: the temples Bali doesn’t have

Bali is Hindu and its temples are living places, busy with offerings every day, but they are mostly modest in scale. For the monumental you go to Central Java. Borobudur was built in the ninth century as a stone mountain of terraces, its walls carved with more than two thousand relief panels and its summit ringed with stupas. Abandoned, buried under volcanic ash and jungle, it was cleared in the nineteenth century and is now the largest Buddhist monument on earth. Prambanan, the great Hindu temple complex of the same era, is an hour away.

The way to see Borobudur is early. Watch the sun come up from the hill at Punthuk Setumbu, with the stupas rising out of the mist and Merapi behind, then go onto the temple as it opens. Access to the upper terraces is now limited to small timed groups, so the places need booking ahead. Add a day in Yogyakarta for the sultan’s palace and the batik workshops, and Prambanan at dusk.

There is one hotel that makes this easy to recommend. Amanjiwo faces Borobudur across the rice fields, so the temple is part of the view from the moment you arrive.

Java works best at the start of a trip, while early mornings are still easy, with Bali as the reward. Flights from Yogyakarta to Bali take a little over an hour. Java and Bali is built exactly this way.

Lombok: the island across the strait

On a clear day you can see Lombok from Bali’s east coast, across the Lombok Strait, with the volcano Rinjani rising behind it. It is roughly the same size as Bali and has a fraction of the development. The south coast in particular is a run of bays and headlands with very little on them, and the three Gili islands lie just off the north-west coast for a day of snorkelling.

Lombok is the right choice for anyone who loves Bali’s landscape but not its crowds, and for families, who get more space for their money. It is a short flight from Bali, or a fast boat for those who prefer the sea.

Sumba: the remote one

Sumba lies to the south-east, an hour’s flight from Bali. It is larger than Bali, with a small fraction of its visitors, and it feels like a different country. The villages of West Sumba are clusters of clan houses with roofs rising into tall thatched peaks, surrounded by megalithic stone tombs that are still used. Horses matter here: in February and March, the Pasola sees mounted horsemen from rival villages ride at each other hurling wooden spears, a ritual tied to the planting season, whose dates are set by village elders.

Sumba is also where you find two of the most interesting beach hotels in Asia, and they could hardly be more different.

Nihi is the one people cross the world for, with a price to match. Cap Karoso on the west coast is newer, lighter on the pocket and close to the old villages. Our Bali and Sumba journey can be built around either.

Which one, then?

If this is your first time in Indonesia and you have ten nights, spend them on Bali, but spend them well: the hills, the east, and the sea, as in Bali, Done Properly. With two weeks, add one island. With three, add two, though Java and Sumba together is a lot of flying for one trip.

And if you want the most complete version of all, Aman has a house in Java and two in Bali. Taken in order they make The Three Amans: the temple, the valley and the sea.

At a glance

  • Java: fly Bali to Yogyakarta, a little over an hour. Three nights. Best April to October.
  • Lombok: a short flight or fast boat from Bali. Three to four nights. Best April to October.
  • Sumba: fly Bali to Tambolaka, about an hour. Three to five nights. Drier from April; very quiet in the green season, January to March.
