China by Train: The Silk Road Express, Explained

China · Xi’an to Dunhuang · 7 min read

China · By Rail

Most first visits to China follow the same three points: Beijing, Xi’an, Shanghai. They are worth it. But the country’s west — the high grassland of Qinghai, the deserts of Gansu, the oasis towns that once supplied the caravans — barely features, because until recently the only comfortable way across it was to fly over it.

The Silk Road Express changes that. Here is what the train is, where it goes, what it costs, and who we think it suits.

— The short answer —

What it is: China’s first international luxury train, 38 private suites for no more than 80 guests, running the old Silk Road through the west of the country.

What it costs: from £4,290 per person for five days, or from £6,200 for seven with two nights in Xi’an first. Meals, drinks, excursions and transfers are included.

Who it suits: travellers who have seen eastern China, or never fancied the cities, and want the landscape that most visitors never reach.

The road that was never one road

The Silk Road was a network, not a highway: a chain of oasis towns strung between deserts and mountains, each a stage on journeys that took months. Goods, faiths and ideas moved along it in both directions for more than a thousand years. Buddhism came into China this way, and the caves at Dunhuang are the record of it.

A train follows that shape well. You cross the land at ground level, stage by stage, and each morning you wake somewhere that looks nothing like the evening before — grassland, then salt flats, then dunes.

From a recent journey

“Endless lowlands, with mountains in the background, some with snow caps. These mountains were growing as we went along. One realises how vast the country is.”

What the train is

Fifteen carriages, painted by hand on the outside by a team from the Tsinghua Academy of Fine Arts with cloud motifs from the Han dynasty, and fitted out inside by Hirsch Bedner Associates, the hotel interior designers. There are 38 suites, every one with its own bathroom, Aesop toiletries and a butler on call around the clock.

Two dining carriages, each with an open kitchen: the Silk Road Kitchen cooks the food of the region the train is crossing, and Lumen is the international room. Both lay their tables with antique British silver on Jingdezhen porcelain. Beyond them are a lounge bar, a 270-degree observation carriage, a mahjong room, a small karaoke room and — something almost no train has — a cigar room for two with its own extraction.

The route

The journey we would choose first is Imperial Splendour, seven days from Xi’an to Dunhuang:

  • Xi’an, two nights. The Terracotta Army, a Tang-dynasty banquet, and a bicycle ride along the top of the old city wall.
  • Xining, reached by first-class high-speed train. Kumbum Monastery, birthplace of the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism, then you board.
  • Chaka Salt Lake, a morning on a white salt mirror at around 3,000 metres.
  • Dunhuang, camels on the Mingsha dunes, the Crescent Moon Spring, and the Mogao Grottoes, including two special-access caves.

It runs the other way too, Dunhuang to Xi’an. Bustling Beginning is the same journey without Xi’an, in five days. Two longer journeys continue west across Xinjiang to Kashgar: Golden Autumn in October, and the seventeen-day Hidden Realms of Xinjiang in early summer.

Five things to know before you go

The altitude. Xining is at about 2,200 metres and Chaka at about 3,000, and the air is thin and dry. Imperial Splendour climbs gradually from Xi’an, which helps; whichever journey you take, go gently on the first day, drink plenty of water and go easy on alcohol at the start. If you have a heart or breathing condition, speak to your doctor before booking.

Mogao needs your passport early. Tickets for the Mogao Grottoes are issued against passport details at least thirty days before your visit. Book inside that window and the caves cannot be promised; the train substitutes another excursion if tickets cannot be secured.

You will be off the grid. There is no Wi-Fi aboard, and across the desert and the plateau very little phone signal. Local life in the north-west also runs on a later rhythm than Beijing, roughly two hours behind, though the clocks show Beijing time.

Set up WeChat before you leave. The team on the ground uses it to keep in touch, and with a bank card added it pays for almost everything on the days off the train. It is far easier to install and set up at home than on arrival.

Check your power bank. Chinese airports are strict: a power bank must be clearly labelled, carry the 3C safety mark and go in your hand luggage, or security will keep it. On board you will not need one — every suite has USB charging.

When to go

The 2027 departures run from late April to October. Summer is the main season; the north-west is dry, with strong sun at altitude and a big difference between day and night temperatures, so pack layers whatever the month. October is the month for colour: the Golden Autumn journey is timed for the weeks when the desert poplar forests of the Tarim turn gold.

Either side of the train

The train is the centre of the journey, not the whole of it. Most of our travellers will want a few days in Beijing or Shanghai first, or more time in Xi’an at their own pace. We arrange the flights and the nights either side around the departure you choose.

Questions we are asked

What is the Silk Road Express?
The Silk Road Express is China’s first international luxury train. It carries no more than 80 guests in 38 private suites, each with its own bathroom, with two dining carriages, an observation carriage and a butler on call around the clock.

Where does the Silk Road Express go?
In 2027 the train runs four journeys in western China. The main route links Xi’an, Xining, Chaka Salt Lake and Dunhuang; in October and early summer, longer journeys continue west across Xinjiang to Kashgar.

How much does the Silk Road Express cost?
Through Lotus & Fairways, the five-day Bustling Beginning is from £4,290 per person sharing a Deluxe Suite, and the seven-day Imperial Splendour, with two nights in Xi’an, from £6,200. The train prices in US dollars, so sterling is confirmed at booking. International flights are priced separately.

What is included on the Silk Road Express?
Your suite on the train and the hotels between, every meal, drinks, guided excursions with entrance fees and VIP access, private transfers, luggage handling and a professional photographer.

Is altitude a problem on the Silk Road Express?
Parts of the route are high: Xining sits at about 2,200 metres and Chaka Salt Lake at about 3,000. Take the first day gently. If you have a heart or breathing condition, speak to your doctor before booking.

When does the Silk Road Express run?
2027 departures run from late April to October, in either direction. The October Golden Autumn journey is timed for the weeks when the desert poplar forests turn gold.
