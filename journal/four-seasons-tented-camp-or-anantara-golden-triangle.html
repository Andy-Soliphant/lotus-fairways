Four Seasons Tented Camp or Anantara Golden Triangle?

Thailand · The Golden Triangle · 5 min read

Thailand · The Golden Triangle

Two of the most asked-about hotels in northern Thailand sit on the same stretch of the Ruak River, look across at the same two countries, and share the same reason for existing: elephants. Most comparisons stop there and call it a draw. It is not a draw. They suit different people, and the difference is simple enough to put in one line.

— The short answer —

Travelling as a couple, or as adults? The Four Seasons Tented Camp. The camp is the holiday.

Travelling with children, or wanting proper rooms? Anantara Golden Triangle. The elephants are at breakfast, and nobody is too young to come.

Both are excellent. It comes down to preference, and to who is travelling.

Where the Golden Triangle actually is

The Golden Triangle is the point where the Ruak River meets the Mekong, and Thailand, Laos and Myanmar meet with them. It is about an hour by road from Chiang Rai airport, and it is quieter than its name suggests: hill country, river, and very little else. That is the appeal. Both properties are built to look out over the three countries, and both keep elephants on their own land.

Four Seasons Tented Camp: when the camp is the holiday

Arrival is by longtail boat up the Ruak, and that is usually the moment guests understand what they have booked. The tents are scattered along half a mile of bamboo hillside, each looking across the river into Laos and Myanmar, with copper baths and outdoor showers under thatch. Bensley designed it, and it shows in the details: nothing is left undecided.

Unusually for Four Seasons, the rate covers everything. Meals, drinks, the spa on its open platform above the valley, the time with the elephants — nobody spends the stay reading a menu or signing a bill. The elephants are rescues, and the hours with them are the reason to come rather than a diversion from it.

Our founder’s verdict

Exit your luxury tent and be amidst the jungle and the elephants. This is a place we love, and love to be at. So will you.

The one thing to know before booking is that it is not a family choice. Older children only — which is exactly why couples love it.

Anantara Golden Triangle: when the family is coming

Sixty-one rooms and suites on a bluff above the point where the three countries meet, with the elephant camp on the same land. It is a resort with proper rooms rather than tents, which suits anyone travelling with children, and anyone who simply prefers walls to canvas.

What it does better than anywhere else is the morning. You wake up, go to breakfast, and the elephants are below you.

Our founder’s verdict

Having your breakfast whilst watching the elephants meander below is one of the most enjoyable simple pleasures I have encountered. And at the Anantara Golden Triangle it can be a daily occurrence.

With any elephant camp, it is right to ask how the animals are cared for before you go. We will tell you exactly how each of these works, and we are happy to be asked.

“Anantara is for families. Four Seasons is tented, and older children only. Both are excellent — it is down to preference.”

How many nights, and what goes either side

Two nights is the least we would suggest at either, and it is how the Four Seasons camp sells its stays. Three is better if you can spare them. Most people arrive from Chiang Mai, and the natural pairing is a few nights in the city first — at Four Seasons Chiang Mai in the rice terraces, or at Rachamankha inside the old city walls. If you are heading north into Laos by river, The Legend Chiang Rai is the hinge before the border.

The Tented Camp is also one of the eight Bensley hotels on The Architect’s Asia, our nineteen-night journey through his work across four countries.

If you are still torn, tell us who is travelling and when. It usually takes one question to settle it.
