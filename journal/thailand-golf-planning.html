Thailand Golf Holidays: How to Plan the Perfect Trip

By Andy, our CEO · Thailand · Golf Guide · 8 min read

Thailand · Golf Guide

A Thailand golf holiday planned well is one of the finest experiences in travel. Planned badly — wrong region, wrong time of year, wrong courses in the wrong order — it is a frustrating exercise in expensive logistics. The gap between the two is usually knowledge, relationships and honest advice from someone who has made both mistakes and learned from them.

I have spent thirty years planning golf trips to Thailand. This is what I know about making them work, written for golfers travelling from the UK.

Start with timing

Thailand is playable year-round — but not everywhere, and not without consequences. The dry season runs broadly from November to April across most of the country, but the monsoon reaches different regions at different times.

Booking the wrong region at the wrong time of year is the single most avoidable mistake in Thailand golf planning. I have seen it happen often enough to make it the first conversation about any set of dates. Thailand is fortunate in having somewhere playable at virtually any time of year — but knowing where that is requires knowing the country well.

Planning from the UK

Most UK golfers fly overnight into Bangkok and land in the morning. The arrival day is for settling in, not for golf: the body clock needs a night, and nobody plays their best round straight off a long-haul flight. The first tee time belongs on day two at the earliest.

Seven to ten nights is the sweet spot. Four or five rounds in a week, with a rest day in the middle, leaves room for the things that make a Thailand trip more than a golf trip. Two regions in one holiday works well — Bangkok with Hua Hin, or Bangkok with Chiang Mai — provided the move between them is planned as part of the trip rather than squeezed in around it.

Travelling with a partner or family? Fourteen nights works perfectly. It gives room for golf without rushing, and for the days everyone shares — a beach, a temple at dawn, a cooking class, a long lunch somewhere nobody else has found. Those are the days that become the memories people take home.

Choose the right courses in the right order

Thailand has hundreds of golf courses. A small number are genuinely great. A larger number are good. Some have reputations that exceed their reality. Knowing which is which — on any given date, with any given group, at any given ability level — requires more than reading a ranking. It requires having played them, having the relationships to get honest information about current conditions, and understanding how courses suit different playing styles and handicap ranges.

Course order matters as much as course selection. Starting with a more forgiving layout before moving to a more demanding one, or sequencing courses so that the visual drama builds across the trip — these things sound like details but they are what separate an itinerary from a list of tee times.

Understand the caddie

Every serious Thailand golf trip should be built around the caddie experience. This is not a nice-to-have — it is the defining quality of golf in Thailand, and the thing that most surprises golfers on their first visit. A good Thai caddie will know your game by the third hole, will improve your score through course management rather than flattery, and will find the ball you were certain was lost.

For many golfers the caddie turns out to be their favourite part of the trip. They add so much for so little. Enjoy the experience — you will miss it when it has gone! Caddie fees and tipping vary by course, and some courses now make buggies compulsory. We brief you on the local norms before you travel, so nothing on the day comes as a surprise.

Returning home and pushing your own trolley afterwards is a genuine psychological adjustment. We hear this regularly from golfers who have played Thailand for the first time. It is, perhaps, the truest measure of what caddies here offer.

What shapes the cost

Three things decide what a Thailand golf holiday costs: the season, the hotel and the courses. Green fees at the best courses rise in the peak months from December to February. The hotel matters more than anything else to how the trip feels, and the gap between a good one and the right one is often smaller than people expect. And a week built around the top courses costs more than one that mixes in good courses at a gentler price — which is sometimes the better trip.

Plan the non-golf days

Thailand's non-golf offering is one of the strongest arguments for choosing it over other Asian golf destinations. Beaches, temples, extraordinary food, night markets, ethical elephant sanctuaries in the north — the days between rounds are as rewarding as the rounds themselves. Non-golfing partners are never left without meaningful things to do, and building those experiences thoughtfully into the itinerary makes the whole trip more satisfying for everyone travelling together.

The combination of golf and culture, rather than golf alone, is what makes the best Thailand trips genuinely memorable rather than merely excellent at the sport.

Questions golfers ask

When is the best time for a golf holiday in Thailand?

For most of the country, November to April. Chiang Mai is at its best from November to February; Phuket and the Andaman coast are wettest from May to October. There is somewhere playable most of the year, but the region has to follow the month.

How many rounds should I plan in a week?

Four or five rounds in a seven-night trip suits most golfers. Leave the arrival day free, and put a rest day in the middle.

How long is the flight from the UK to Thailand?

Direct flights from London to Bangkok take around eleven to twelve hours. Thailand is seven hours ahead of the UK in winter and six in summer.

Do I need a caddie in Thailand?

Caddies are standard at almost every good course in Thailand, and they are often a golfer’s favourite part of the trip — they add so much for so little. Tipping is expected; we brief you on the local norm before you travel.

Which region is best for a first Thailand golf holiday?

Bangkok and Hua Hin are the easiest starting points: short transfers from the airport, a strong choice of courses and good hotels. Chiang Mai and Phuket suit a second trip, or a week that combines golf with somewhere very different.
