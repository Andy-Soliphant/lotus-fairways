Trains in Vietnam: Which Ones Are Worth Taking

Vietnam · By Rail · 7 min read

Vietnam · By Rail

Ten years ago the honest advice about trains in Vietnam was simple: take one short scenic stretch for the experience, and fly everything else. That has changed. There is now a proper luxury train running the length of the country, a private carriage on the central coast, and a heritage service over the most beautiful part of the line. Here is what each one is, and which we would actually book.

— The short answer —

The whole country: SJourney, Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City in seven nights, from £7,750 per person.

One beautiful day: the coast between Hue and Da Nang, or The Vietage between Da Nang, Quy Nhon and Nha Trang.

The mountains: the night train to Sapa works, but it is not the highlight. A car is often better.

The line itself

Almost every train journey in Vietnam uses the same single track: the North–South Railway, which the French completed in 1936. It runs some 1,700 kilometres between Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, and for much of that distance it sits between the mountains and the sea. The ordinary service, the Reunification Express, takes around 30 to 35 hours end to end. It is how many Vietnamese families still travel, and it is an experience, but it is not a comfortable way to see the country. What has changed is what now runs on that track.

1. SJourney: the whole country in a week

SJourney is Vietnam’s first true luxury train. It runs from Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City, or the reverse, in eight days and seven nights. You have a private cabin or suite for the whole journey, a dining car with its own chefs, a bar and a cabin attendant. The train stops along the way and you leave it with a guide: Ninh Binh, the caves of Quang Binh, the imperial city of Hue, Hoi An, and the quiet southern coast. Then you rejoin it and move on. You unpack once.

It is the only option here that replaces the whole trip rather than one day of it. There are also two four-day journeys: the southern coast between Ho Chi Minh City and Hoi An, and a loop from Hanoi into the mountains at Sapa and out to Ha Long Bay. 2. The Vietage by Anantara: a day on the central coast

The Vietage is a single private carriage, restored and fitted out by Anantara, that is attached to the regular train. It holds a maximum of twelve guests in six booths for two. It runs between Da Nang and Quy Nhon, with lunch, and between Quy Nhon and Nha Trang, with afternoon tea; each leg takes five to six hours. Drinks are free-flowing and there is a short head and shoulder massage on board.

It is a lovely way to make a transfer that you would otherwise do by road or a short flight, and Quy Nhon is a part of the coast that few visitors reach. Two things to know: because the carriage rides on the ordinary train, it keeps the ordinary train’s timetable, delays included; and the seats are fixed, so it is a day of looking out of the window rather than a hotel on wheels. Published 2026 fares are around US$450 per person a leg. We include it within a journey rather than selling it alone.

3. Hue to Da Nang: the most beautiful three hours

If you take only one ordinary train in Vietnam, take this one. Between Hue and Da Nang the line leaves the road behind and runs along the cliffs of the Hai Van headland, with the sea directly below the window and the lagoon at Lang Co on the way. Since March 2024 Vietnam Railways has run a dedicated heritage service on the route, the Central Heritage Connection, with a carriage given over to music from Hue and Da Nang and regional food served with its story. The journey takes about three hours.

It is comfortable, not luxurious, and we arrange it as a half day within a private journey, with a car waiting at the other end. The alternative is to drive over the Hai Van Pass itself, which is also spectacular. On a clear day we would do the train one way and the road the other.

4. The night train to Sapa

The night train from Hanoi to Lao Cai, on the border with China, is how Sapa was reached for decades. Several companies run private carriages attached to it, with two- and four-berth cabins, and the journey takes around eight hours overnight. From Lao Cai it is about another hour by road up into the mountains.

It is clean and private, but there are no showers and the sleep is broken. Since the expressway opened, a private car from Hanoi takes about five to six hours door to door, and most of our guests now prefer it. If the idea of the train appeals, SJourney’s four-day loop to Sapa and Ha Long Bay is a more comfortable way to have it.

How we would use them

For a first visit, the cleanest answer is SJourney with a few nights either side: arrive early in Hanoi at the Metropole, take the train south, then stay on at The Reverie or fly to a beach such as Amanoi. For a journey built around the centre of the country, we would use the Hue to Da Nang line for a morning, stay at Four Seasons The Nam Hai near Hoi An, and take The Vietage south to Quy Nhon. Either way, the train becomes part of the trip rather than a way of getting through it. Further south, Belmond’s Eastern & Oriental Express runs from Singapore through Malaysia; see all our rail journeys in Asia.

The central coast is at its wettest from roughly September to December, and the Hai Van section is the stretch most affected by storms. February to April suits the whole length of the country.

Questions we are asked

Is there a luxury train in Vietnam?

Yes. SJourney runs the full length of the country between Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City in eight days and seven nights, with private cabins and suites, a dining car and guided stops. The Vietage by Anantara is a single private carriage on the central coast, running day journeys between Da Nang, Quy Nhon and Nha Trang.

What is the best train journey in Vietnam?

For the whole country, SJourney, because it covers Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City with every stop arranged. For a single scenic day, the coast between Hue and Da Nang, where the line runs along the cliffs of the Hai Van headland above the sea.

How long does the train take from Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City?

On the ordinary Reunification Express it is around 30 to 35 hours without stopping. SJourney takes eight days and seven nights because it stops along the way, with a guide, at Ninh Binh, Hue, Hoi An and the southern coast.

Is the night train from Hanoi to Sapa worth it?

It is comfortable rather than luxurious: a private two-berth cabin in a carriage attached to the night train, around eight hours to Lao Cai, then an hour by road up to Sapa. Many travellers now prefer a private car on the expressway, which takes about five to six hours door to door.
