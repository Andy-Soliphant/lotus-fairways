City·Camp·River·Town·Temple·Jungle·Coast
Seven kinds of place. One imagination.

Most hotels are decorated. Bill Bensley's are written. Each one begins with a story — a fictional collector, a lost expedition, a family that never existed — and then the building, the gardens, the furniture and the uniforms are all made to serve it. He has worked out of Bangkok for decades, and the results are scattered across Southeast Asia in places most itineraries pass at speed.

You cannot see the range of it in one hotel. A single stay reads as extravagance. Six of them, in sequence, read as an argument — because the same imagination produces a prince's townhouse on the Chao Phraya, a camp where elephants walk past the tent at dawn, and a jungle lodge built on land that was going to be logged until the camp bought the concession instead.

This journey is not about luxury. It is about what happens when one person is allowed to finish the thought.

The Journey

1Bangkok

The Collector — Two Nights on the River

The group gathers at The Siam, on the Chao Phraya in quiet Dusit rather than the hotel district. Black and white, art deco, and the owning family's collection of antiques through every public room. It is the clearest statement of the house style you will see all fortnight, and the right place to begin an argument about one man's work.

Chao PhrayaThe Siam
2Golden Triangle

The Camp — Two Nights Above Three Borders

Tents in the hills where Thailand, Laos and Myanmar meet, looking out over the Mekong. Canvas, brass and campaign furniture, pitched as though an expedition arrived and never left. The elephants here are the reason most people come, and the reason to be careful about how that is arranged — we will tell you exactly what happens before you book.

Four Seasons Tented Camp
3The Mekong

The River — Three Nights Downstream

The one leg not of his making, and the journey needs it. Three days on the water between the camp and Luang Prabang, through gorges and villages with no road to them, at the speed the river decides. It is the pause between two very loud rooms, and by the end of it the group knows each other.

MekongRiver cruise
4Luang Prabang

The Town — Two Nights in the Hills

Tents and villas around a waterfall outside the old royal capital, French colonial by way of a nineteenth-century explorer's imagination. Luang Prabang itself is a UNESCO town of monasteries and morning alms, and the contrast between the two is the point of stopping here.

Rosewood Luang Prabang
5Siem Reap

The Temples — Two Nights, Ten Villas

Ten pool villas in their own enclave, taken in full by this group — which is the whole of it, so the enclave is yours. Angkor in the morning, and an afternoon to understand why a designer who builds fantasies chose to build a foundation here as well.

Bensley Collection Pool Villas
6Cardamom Mountains

The Wild — Three Nights, and You Arrive by Zipline

There is no road in. You come down a wire over the river to the camp, and the tents are strung along the water below. The land was scheduled for logging until the camp took on the concession; rangers patrol from here, and you can go out with them. It is the most theatrical arrival on the route and the most serious thing on it, at the same time.

Preak TachanShinta Mani Wild
7Da Nang

The Coast — Two Nights, and the Sea

Cut into the Son Tra peninsula across four terraces in black and white, with a funicular running down through them to the sand. After the Cardamoms it is somewhere to swim, eat well and let sixteen nights settle. The last of the six, and the one that sends you home slowly.
