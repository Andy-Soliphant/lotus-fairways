For three centuries the Cham controlled the trade in spices and silk between China, India, the islands of Indonesia and Abbasid Baghdad. They were seafarers, and when it suited them, pirates. Their kingdom ran down the coast of what is now central Vietnam from the second century until the fifteenth — brick and sandstone towers, Shiva, Sanskrit, and a script they invented for their own language. The oldest surviving text in any Southeast Asian language is Cham.

Then it went. Vijaya, the capital, fell in 1471 — sixty thousand killed, thirty thousand taken as slaves, the city obliterated. The last remnant held on until 1832. What survives is a scatter of towers along the Vietnamese coast, the finest collection of Cham sculpture in the world in a museum in Da Nang, and communities still living, still Cham, in Ninh Thuan and along the Cambodian Mekong.

Most travellers pass the towers on the road to somewhere else. This journey is the somewhere else.

The Journey

1Bangkok

The Welcome — Two Nights on the River

The group gathers on the Chao Phraya. A day on the water and out to Ayutthaya, the trading capital the Cham knew from the other end of the voyage — a Cham inscription records Siamese merchants sailing to Champa, alongside Khmer, Javanese and Bengali traders. Dinner together, and the shape of the fortnight ahead.

Chao PhrayaCapella Bangkok
2Siem Reap

The War — Two Nights at Angkor

The Bayon carries the record of it in stone: Cham warships driven up the Mekong into the Tonlé Sap, the Khmer king killed, Angkor sacked. They fought with Chinese siege crossbows, taught to them six years earlier. Jayavarman VII drove them out in 1181. Every visitor to Angkor walks past those panels. You will arrive knowing exactly what you are looking at.

Raffles Grand Hotel d'Angkor
3Phnom Penh

The Exile — Two Nights on the Mekong

A full day on the river to the Cham communities who have lived along it since their kingdom fell — Muslim, Cham-speaking, and still here. This is the quietest part of the journey and, for most people, the one they talk about afterwards.

MekongRaffles Hotel Le Royal
4Can Tho

The Crossing — One Night in the Delta

Down the Mekong and across the border by boat, the way the Cham went when they left. A night in the delta before the coast — the only transfer on this journey that is also the point of it.

Mekong crossing
5Ninh Thuan

The Survivors — Three Nights on the Coast

Po Klong Garai, still in active worship after seven centuries, raised by the king who traded two provinces for a Vietnamese princess and died months after the wedding. Po Nagar at Nha Trang, burned by Javanese raiders in 774. And two gravestones cut in Kufic Arabic, dated 1039, marking the tomb of a merchant named Abu Kamil — buried here nine hundred years before anyone called this place Vietnam. The largest Cham population in the country still lives in these villages, Hindu and Muslim both.

Amanoi, Vinh Hy Bay
6Quy Nhon

The Last Capital — Two Nights

Vijaya, and the towers at Banh It standing above the rice. This is where it ended in 1471, and almost nobody comes.

Anantara Quy Nhon Villas
7Hoi An

The Sacred Heart — Three Nights, and the Sea

My Son at first light, before the gates open to anyone else — the sanctuary where Champa began and where, after everything else, this journey ends. Then the Museum of Cham Sculpture in Da Nang, and two days on the South China Sea: the sea they ruled, and time enough to let fifteen nights settle before anyone flies home.
