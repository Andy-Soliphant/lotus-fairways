If you have never been to Vietnam, this is the journey we would send you on first. It runs the length of the country from north to south, touches each of the places that make it what it is, and gives you enough time in each that you remember them separately.

It is built as much around what you do as where you sleep: the back streets of Hanoi, a night aboard among the islands, the imperial city of Hue, a morning at a Hoi An cooking school, and the Saigon river to the tunnels at Cu Chi.

The Journey

1Hanoi

The Capital — Two Nights

Two nights at La Siesta Premium Hang Be, in the Old Quarter a few steps from Hoan Kiem Lake. A day by open jeep: first the back streets, homes and markets most visitors never find, then the mausoleum, Tran Quoc pagoda, the Temple of Literature and the Long Bien bridge, and finally the quieter villages around West Lake and Truc Bach, with lunch along the way.

La Siesta Premium Hang Be
2Lan Ha Bay

The Islands — One Night Aboard

To the coast and a Junior Suite aboard Heritage Line’s Ginger, a twelve-suite ship with its restaurant glassed in on nearly every side. By bicycle on Cat Ba island to Viet Hai, a village of eighty households that can only be reached from the sea; a cooking class aboard as the light goes; and a morning swim or kayak among the cliffs before breakfast.

Lan Ha BayHeritage Line Ginger
3Hue

The Imperial City — Two Nights

A flight south to Pilgrimage Village, in gardens fifteen minutes outside the city. The Nguyen emperors’ citadel and the tomb of Minh Mang, lunch in a garden house, then an afternoon by bicycle along the Nhu Y river through old villages to the Chuon lagoon, and out onto it by boat to the stilt houses of the families who live on the water.

Chuon LagoonPilgrimage Village
4Hoi An

The Old Town — Three Nights

Over the Hai Van Pass by road to Boutique Hoi An Resort, on the beach. A morning at the market with a chef, then by boat to the Red Bridge cooking school to cook and eat what you bought. A day that is yours.

Thu BonBoutique Hoi An Resort
5Saigon

The South — Three Nights

A flight to Mai House, a short walk from the cathedral and the Post Office. Up the Saigon river by boat to the tunnels at Cu Chi, and a day on the back of a vintage Vespa: coffee, the flower markets, the temples of Cholon, and the Independence Palace to finish.

Saigon RiverMai House Saigon
