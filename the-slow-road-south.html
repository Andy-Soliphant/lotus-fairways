Most journeys through Southeast Asia are built to be efficient: a city, a temple, a beach, a flight home. This one is built the other way round. It crosses into Laos by river rather than air, goes down the Vietnamese coast by train, and stops long enough in each place that you stop being a visitor to it.

It is a journey we have run in full, and it is the most complete picture of the region we know how to give. Four countries in order, north to south: the temples and the mountains of northern Thailand, the slow river into Laos, Angkor and the hard history of Phnom Penh, and then the whole length of Vietnam, from the Red Dao villages above Sapa to the floating markets of the Mekong.

Forty-four nights, and none of them spent in transit.

The Journey

1Bangkok

The River — Three Nights

Arrive in your own time at Four Seasons Bangkok on the Chao Phraya. A morning on the klongs by private longtail, through the canal-side neighbourhoods that were the city’s streets before it had roads, to the Grand Palace, Wat Pho and Wat Arun. On another evening, a tuk-tuk once the heat has gone: Chinatown’s food stalls, the flower market at Pak Khlong Talat, Wat Pho lit up and nearly empty, and a last drink above the city.

Chao PhrayaFour Seasons Bangkok
2Chiang Mai

The Mountain — Three Nights

North to 137 Pillars House, in the Rajah Brooke Suite. A full day on Doi Inthanon, Thailand’s highest mountain: the summit cloud forest, the two royal chedis, then down through the Pha Dok Siew waterfalls on a trail built and kept by the Karen village at its foot, where the coffee is grown and poured. Then the old city by red truck, khao soi for lunch, and Warorot market.

137 Pillars House
3Chiang Rai

The White Temple — One Night

Through the mountains to The Legend on the Mae Kok, stopping at Wat Rong Khun — the White Temple, the life’s work of the painter Chalermchai Kositpipat, and like nothing else in Thailand.

The Legend Chiang Rai
4Pakbeng

The Slow Boat — One Night on the Mekong

Across the border at Houay Xay and onto the river. Two days of slow boat down the Mekong into Laos, with a stop at a Khmu village of sixty families, and a night at Le Grand Pakbeng in the jungle above the water, halfway to Luang Prabang.

MekongLe Grand Pakbeng
5Luang Prabang

The Royal Town — Four Nights

The river delivers you, by way of the Pak Ou caves and their thousands of Buddhas. Rosewood Luang Prabang sits in the hills outside town. The morning market, Wat Mai, the old royal palace and Wat Xieng Thong; lunch at the Kuang Si falls and its bear sanctuary; a day walking through Khmu and Hmong villages and back down the Nam Khan by boat. And a day that is simply yours.

Nam KhanRosewood Luang Prabang
6Siem Reap

Angkor — Three Nights

A direct flight south to Phum Baitang and its villas in the rice fields. Angkor Wat before dawn, into the temple as the crowds leave for breakfast; then Angkor Thom, the faces of the Bayon, and Ta Prohm with the jungle growing through it. An evening on the back of a Vespa through Siem Reap’s night markets and street kitchens.

Phum Baitang
7Phnom Penh

The Capital — Two Nights

By road on the old Angkor highway, by way of Sambor Prei Kuk — brick temples a century older than Angkor, with almost no one at them. Two nights at Raffles Hotel Le Royal. A morning at Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek, which not everyone will want and which explains the country better than anything else can; and a gentler one by cyclo, past Wat Phnom, the central market and the Royal Palace.

Raffles Hotel Le Royal
8Hanoi

The Old Quarter — Three Nights

North to Capella Hanoi. A day by jeep through the alleys most visitors never find, the Temple of Literature and Tran Quoc pagoda, and the villages around West Lake. A tour of the Opera House, next door, subject to its reopening after renovation.

Capella Hanoi
9Sapa

The Mountains — Three Nights

Into the Hoang Lien mountains and a pool villa at Topas Ecolodge, looking across the valley. Walks to the Red Dao villages of Lech, Nam Nhiu and Nam Cang, through rice terraces and over the river, with time for a Red Dao herbal bath at the lodge. The third day is free.

Topas Ecolodge
10Ninh Binh

The Karsts — Two Nights

Down from the mountains to Emeralda Ninh Binh. Hoa Lu, Vietnam’s first capital, then a private sampan along the Tam Coc river between the limestone towers, and the climb to the cave pagodas on Ngu Nhac mountain for those who want it.

Emeralda Ninh Binh
11Ha Long Bay

The Bay — One Night Aboard

The Imperial Suite on Heritage Line’s Violet. By bamboo boat through the floating village at Cua Van, into Tien Ong cave, a cooking class as the light goes, and a swim or a climb at Ti Top island in the morning.

Ha Long BayHeritage Line Violet
12Hue

The Imperial City — Three Nights

A flight south to Azerai La Residence, on the Perfume River. Thien Mu pagoda, the tomb of Khai Dinh, the Nguyen emperors’ citadel, and a cyclo to the lotus lake where they came to rest. And a day of Hue’s royal cooking, from the market at Dong Ba to a garden house of the kind found only in this city.

Perfume RiverAzerai La Residence Hue
13Hoi An

The Coast — Five Nights

Over the Hai Van Pass by road to Four Seasons The Nam Hai and a one-bedroom ocean-view villa. A morning on the Thu Bon with the fishermen at the river mouth, then by coracle into the mangroves. The rest is deliberately unplanned: the old town, the beach, and nothing that has to be done.

Thu BonFour Seasons The Nam Hai
14Quy Nhon

The Quiet Bay — Four Nights

South by train from Da Nang, six hours down the coast in a private cabin — the transfer as part of the journey. A hill pool villa at Zannier Bai San Ho, above a bay very few people from Britain have heard of. Four nights with nothing in them, on purpose.

Zannier Bai San Ho
15Saigon

The City — Three Nights

A flight to The Reverie on Nguyen Hue. Up the Saigon river by boat to the tunnels at Cu Chi, and a day on the back of a vintage Vespa: coffee the Vietnamese way, the bird cafés, Cholon’s temples, the Post Office and the Independence Palace.

Saigon RiverThe Reverie Saigon
16Can Tho

The Delta — Three Nights

Into the Mekong Delta, and the last stretch by boat to Legacy Mekong on its own islet in the Hau river. The Binh Thuy temple, the Duong family house of 1870 where The Lover was filmed, and Nam Nha pagoda; then the Cai Rang floating market at dawn, with breakfast on board. From here, home by way of Saigon.

Hau RiverLegacy Mekong
