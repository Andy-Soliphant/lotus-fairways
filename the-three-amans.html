Aman has three houses in Indonesia, and taken in order they make a complete journey: the temple, the valley and the sea. It is the collector’s route. Some people come for the hotels and find the country; others come for the country and find they have stayed somewhere they will measure everything else against.

Amanjiwo faces Borobudur. Amandari was Aman’s first house in Bali and is still the reference point for the island. Amankila sits on the cliffs above the Lombok Strait with its three pools stepping down to the sea. Four nights at each, in the suites with their own pools, and a private guide with you from the first morning to the last.

The Journey

1Borobudur

The Temple — Four Nights

Yogyakarta, and up into the hills to Amanjiwo, where the rotunda frames Borobudur across the rice fields. Before dawn, to the hill at Punthuk Setumbu to watch the temple appear out of the mist below Merapi, then onto the terraces as they open, with the limited upper-terrace places booked ahead. Prambanan at dusk. A day in Yogyakarta with the kraton and the batik makers, and a day on the estate, which is reason enough to be there.

2Ubud

The Valley — Four Nights

A short flight to Bali and up to Amandari, its suites built like village compounds above the Ayung gorge. A blessing at the village temple next door, Tirta Empul early, the terraces at Jatiluwih, and a walk down into the gorge to the river. Then nothing, which Amandari does better than anywhere.

Ayung GorgeAmandari
3Manggis

The Sea — Four Nights

East to Amankila, on the cliffs above the Lombok Strait. Besakih, the mother temple, high on the slopes of Agung; the water palace at Tirta Gangga; the old walled village of Tenganan. A morning along the coast by outrigger, and the beach club below the cliffs for the afternoons. Ninety minutes to the airport at the end.
