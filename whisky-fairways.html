The Idea

Two Highland traditions, read as one

Golf and whisky grew up on the same ground in Scotland, shaped by the same weather and the same patience. Royal Dornoch and Nairn were laid out within a few miles of distilleries that have been filling casks since before either course had a clubhouse. We've never understood why the two are usually sold as separate holidays.

Whisky & Fairways is built for a private group of six to eight — friends, a society, or a family split across two generations — travelling together with one chauffeured itinerary, one local fixer on call throughout, and evenings built around a tasting that actually connects to where you played that day, not a generic flight of whatever the hotel bar has open.

Dalwhinnie, Scotland's highest distillery, sits almost exactly halfway between Edinburgh and the far north links — which is where day two of this itinerary happens to take you.

The Journey

Five days, built around four rounds and three distillery visits. Courses and casks below are our default routing — every detail can be rebuilt around your group's handicaps and tastes.

01
Edinburgh
Arrival & Dean Village

A late-afternoon arrival, bags taken care of, then a short walk down to Dean Village along the Water of Leith before dinner — the last city evening before five days in the Highlands.

Welcome Dram
Glenkinchie 12
A Lowland malt, lighter and grassier — the gentlest possible introduction before the Highlands take over.
02
Cairngorms & Speyside
North to Dalwhinnie, round at Boat of Garten

The drive north crosses the Cairngorms proper. We stop at Dalwhinnie — 1,164 feet up, the highest distillery in Scotland — for a private tour before an afternoon round at Boat of Garten, a heather-lined James Braid course that rarely sees a visitor from outside Scotland.

Evening Dram
Dalwhinnie 15, cask strength
Honeyed and heathery, tasted a few hours after standing in the warehouse where it was filled.
03
The Moray Coast
Nairn Golf Club

A morning round at Nairn, a genuine championship links along the Moray Firth, dry underfoot even when the rest of Scotland isn't. The afternoon is free — a second nine, a walk on the beach, or simply a long lunch looking out at the water.

Evening Dram
Benromach 10
A working Speyside distillery a few miles inland — lightly peated, which surprises most people who think Speyside means unpeated by default.
04
Sutherland
Royal Dornoch

The reason most of this group booked the trip. Royal Dornoch's Championship Course has sat in the world's top ten links for decades, and it's still one of the few where you can walk on without six months' notice — because we ask well in advance, on your behalf.

Evening Dram
Old Pulteney 12
From Wick, further north still — maritime, briny, a fitting close to the golf leg of the trip.
05
Glencoe & Departure
The road west, via Glenfinnan

Clubs packed away for the drive south-west, over the Glenfinnan Viaduct and down through Glencoe — some of the most photographed road in Scotland, and rightly so. A final tasting flight over lunch before onward travel from Glasgow or Fort William.

Farewell Flight
A dram from each stop, revisited
The full week in one flight — Glenkinchie through Old Pulteney, tasted back to back.
